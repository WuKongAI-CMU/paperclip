# DearMe Product Marketing Packaging Guide

Date: 2026-05-07
Audience: product, brand, growth, design, engineering
Scope: Use Polsia's product packaging lessons to guide DearMe's product development and launch narrative.

## Executive Decision

DearMe should not be packaged as an AI writing tool, a personal CRM, a dashboard, or an agent control plane.

DearMe should be packaged as:

> Your personal brand growth team.

The core emotional win is the one Peter noticed: users feel impressed when they can see an AI team working for them. That feeling is not secondary decoration. It is the product.

Polsia's strongest marketing insight is that it makes autonomy visible. It does not only say "AI runs your company"; it shows live agents planning, coding, writing, emailing, pausing ads, and reporting results. DearMe needs the same proof loop for one person's reputation, opportunities, and public presence.

## What Polsia Got Right

### 1. Category Promise Before Feature Promise

Polsia's promise is easy to repeat:

- "AI that runs your company while you sleep."
- "Your autonomous AI co-founder."
- "No employees. No sleep. No limits."

That is a category promise, not a feature list. The user buys a new mental model: I have a company that keeps moving when I am away.

DearMe's equivalent promise should be:

- "Your personal brand growth team."
- "Wake up to a stronger public self."
- "A small AI team that turns your work into opportunities."
- "Your proof, voice, and network, compounded daily."

Avoid leading with:

- "AI content generator"
- "personal CRM"
- "multi-agent platform"
- "autonomous workflow dashboard"
- "Paperclip / OpenClaw / Codex"

Those are substrate or tool categories. They shrink the product.

### 2. The AI Team Is The Interface

Polsia packages capabilities as roles:

- CEO / Strategy
- Engineering
- Marketing / Growth
- Comms
- Ops / Support
- Meta Ads Manager

The user does not inspect a runtime. The user watches a team. That is why the product feels bigger than the underlying stack.

DearMe should make these roles first-class:

- Chief of Staff: decides the daily plan and writes the daily letter.
- Brand Strategist: keeps positioning, audience, and themes coherent.
- Voice Editor: protects the user's tone and blocks generic AI slop.
- Content Producer: turns raw material into posts, essays, newsletters, clips, and portfolio updates.
- Opportunity Scout: finds podcasts, jobs, customers, collaborators, and warm openings.
- Portfolio Builder: maintains public proof pages and case studies.
- Growth Analyst: explains what worked and what to do next.

These should not be tabs named after internal modules. They should feel like colleagues with responsibility, current work, and output.

### 3. Live Proof Beats Claims

Polsia's public live dashboard is a marketing machine. On 2026-05-07, its public API exposed:

- ARR: $8.33M
- Active companies: 6,963
- Total companies: 94,159
- Tasks completed: 803,054
- Messages sent: 1,636,348
- Companies created in 24h: 1,395
- Tasks completed in 24h: 14,093

The point is not only the numbers. The stronger point is that visitors can watch agent actions stream by. It turns product operation into social proof.

DearMe should not copy this literally with personal data. Personal brand work is more sensitive than company-operations demos. But DearMe still needs a proof surface:

- Private default: "Dear me, here's what your team did while you were away."
- Public optional demo: anonymized/redacted brand-building activity.
- Founder dogfood feed: Peter's own brand team working in public.
- Customer proof pages: approved before/after artifacts, not raw private work.

The visual goal: a new visitor should understand in 10 seconds that DearMe is a real team producing real assets, not a blank chatbot.

### 4. Five-Minute WOW Is The Conversion Engine

Polsia's onboarding compresses the first magic moment:

1. Google sign-in.
2. One idea input.
3. Immediate dashboard.
4. AI starts working before the user finishes mentally evaluating the product.
5. Within minutes: name, market research, starter tasks, live site, mood updates, reports.

DearMe's first five minutes should do the personal-brand equivalent:

1. Sign in.
2. Ask one question: "What do you want to become known for?"
3. Optional imports, not required setup.
4. Immediately show the team starting work.
5. Within 90 seconds, show:
   - a draft voice profile,
   - three starter posts,
   - one opportunity lead,
   - one portfolio/profile improvement,
   - one daily plan from the Chief of Staff.

Do not start with settings, connectors, pricing, or a blank dashboard.

### 5. Ritual Creates Retention

Polsia's daily cycle is a habit loop: plan, work, recap.

DearMe should turn this into a personal ritual:

- Morning: "Dear me, today your team is focusing on..."
- During day: approval queue for high-leverage actions.
- Evening: "Dear me, here's what changed in your public surface."
- Weekly: "Your brand is compounding in these three ways."

The product name is a gift. "Dear me" should become the recurring letter format, not just the brand name.

### 6. The Founder Story Is A Distribution Channel

Polsia's founder dogfoods the system and lets the product's work become the story. "The AI is raising its own round" is a meme, not a normal feature announcement.

DearMe's founder story should be equally simple:

> Peter is using DearMe to build the public proof, writing cadence, and opportunity pipeline around his own work.

Launch should not sound like "we built a personal branding app." It should sound like:

> I hired an AI brand team and watched it turn my scattered work into a public operating system.

## DearMe Positioning

### Primary Position

DearMe is a personal brand growth team for ambitious solo operators.

It turns raw work, scattered notes, voice, proof, and relationships into a compounding public presence: posts, pages, essays, outreach, opportunity leads, and weekly strategy.

### Category Frame

DearMe is closer to a managed growth team than to a writing assistant.

The right comparison set:

- a brand strategist,
- a content producer,
- a chief of staff,
- a portfolio manager,
- an opportunity scout.

The wrong comparison set:

- ChatGPT,
- Buffer,
- Notion,
- a generic CRM,
- an agent dashboard.

### Target User

DearMe is for people whose work deserves more surface area:

- founders who need their work to travel,
- solo consultants and agency owners,
- job seekers with strong proof but weak packaging,
- technical builders who do not publish enough,
- creators with expertise but inconsistent output,
- operators whose network should become an opportunity pipeline.

### Core Promise

DearMe makes the user feel:

> I finally have a small team making my reputation compound every day.

That feeling matters more than any isolated feature.

## Product Packaging Requirements

### First Screen

The first screen after onboarding should not be a dashboard of empty metrics.

It should show:

- the user's team roster,
- what each agent is doing now,
- the first artifacts being produced,
- the approval queue,
- the first daily letter,
- visible progress states with human-readable verbs.

Recommended first-screen hierarchy:

1. "Dear me, your team is working."
2. Active work stream.
3. Approval queue.
4. Today's plan.
5. Team roster.
6. Proof metrics.

Do not lead with:

- adapter status,
- model provider,
- API keys,
- raw logs,
- database records,
- generic chat input.

### Agent Work Stream

Every agent action should answer:

- Who is working?
- What are they doing?
- Why does it matter?
- What artifact will I get?
- Do I need to approve anything?

Good examples:

- "Voice Editor is comparing this draft against your approved writing samples."
- "Opportunity Scout found 7 podcasts that recently booked founders like you."
- "Portfolio Builder is turning yesterday's shipped feature into a public proof card."
- "Chief of Staff is reducing today's plan to 3 moves because you have only 20 minutes."

Bad examples:

- "Running workflow..."
- "Calling model..."
- "Processing..."
- "Task completed."
- "Agent execution started."

### Mood And Personality

Polsia's mood face system makes agent work feel alive. DearMe should use the same principle but make it calmer and more professional.

Recommended mood vocabulary:

- Studying your voice
- Sharpening the angle
- Protecting your tone
- Finding openings
- Preparing for approval
- Turning work into proof
- Reducing noise
- Ready for your call

Avoid silly or overly gamified moods. DearMe sells trust, not novelty.

### Approval Queue

For DearMe, approval is the trust boundary.

Default behavior:

- Prepare posts, emails, outreach, and profile changes.
- Ask for approval before publishing or sending.
- Show what changed and why.
- Keep one-click approve, edit, reject, and "make it more like me."

Marketing copy should emphasize:

> Your team prepares the moves. You approve what represents you.

This solves the fear Polsia users voiced on Product Hunt: autonomy is exciting, but loss of control is scary.

### Metrics

Polsia uses company-level traction: ARR, companies, tasks, emails, ads.

DearMe needs personal-brand compounding metrics:

- approved artifacts,
- posts published,
- voice-match score,
- profile/portfolio visits,
- replies and warm intros,
- opportunities found,
- opportunities accepted,
- audience growth,
- proof cards created,
- weekly consistency streak.

Avoid vanity-only metrics. Followers matter, but DearMe should frame growth as opportunity creation.

## Landing Page Guidance

### Recommended Hero

H1:

> Your personal brand growth team.

Subhead:

> DearMe turns your work, voice, and proof into posts, pages, outreach, and opportunities while you live your actual life. Your team prepares the moves. You approve what represents you.

Primary CTA:

> Start your first cycle

Secondary CTA:

> Watch a sample team work

Trust line:

> No posting without approval by default.

### Alternate Hero Lines

- "Wake up to a stronger public self."
- "A small AI team for your reputation."
- "Turn your work into opportunities every day."
- "Your proof deserves a team."
- "Stop starting from a blank page. Start with a team."

### Lines To Avoid

- "Automate your personal brand."
- "Generate viral content with AI."
- "Multi-agent personal CRM."
- "AI writes posts for you."
- "Manage your agents from one dashboard."

Those make DearMe feel either spammy or technical.

### Homepage Sections

Recommended page order:

1. Hero with active team visual, not abstract illustration.
2. Live sample: agents producing posts, proof cards, and opportunity leads.
3. Before/after: scattered work -> packaged public proof.
4. Meet your team: seven roles with concrete outputs.
5. Approval and voice safety: "sounds like you or it does not ship."
6. Daily letter ritual.
7. Founder dogfood / public build proof.
8. Pricing.

Do not make the page a generic SaaS grid of features.

## Onboarding Guidance

### One-Question Start

Ask:

> What do you want to become known for?

Help text:

> A sentence is enough. DearMe will draft your strategy, voice profile, first posts, and opportunity map.

Examples:

- "I'm a technical founder building local-first AI tools."
- "I help real estate agents convert inspection anxiety into confident buying decisions."
- "I'm a designer trying to become known for calm, high-converting B2B SaaS interfaces."

### Optional Imports

Imports should be framed as acceleration, not setup debt:

- website,
- LinkedIn,
- X/Twitter,
- Substack,
- GitHub,
- resume,
- writing samples,
- calendar availability.

Copy:

> Skip this for now. Your team can start from your description.

### First 90 Seconds

The system should visibly produce:

- "Voice profile draft"
- "Audience angle"
- "3 starter posts"
- "1 portfolio proof card"
- "5 opportunity leads"

Even if the deeper automation is still improving, this choreography must be real enough to make the user feel motion.

## Product Development Priorities

### P0: Make The Team Visible

Build these before deep optimization:

1. Team roster with named roles.
2. Live work stream with readable actions.
3. First-cycle generator.
4. Approval queue.
5. Daily letter.
6. Voice profile card.
7. Sample/demo mode that does not require real social account connection.

If users cannot see the team working, DearMe loses its main packaging advantage.

### P1: Make Work Feel Personally Accurate

Add:

- voice samples,
- voice-match scoring,
- forbidden phrases,
- approved phrasing memory,
- "make this more like me",
- content provenance: which note/work artifact produced this output.

This is DearMe's equivalent of Polsia's business memory.

### P2: Make Distribution Real

Add connectors only after the visible loop works:

- X/Twitter draft publishing,
- LinkedIn draft/export,
- newsletter draft,
- website/portfolio publishing,
- outreach email drafts,
- calendar/opportunity follow-up.

Publishing should remain approval-gated by default.

### P3: Public Proof Flywheel

Add:

- opt-in public live demo,
- public portfolio examples,
- founder dogfood feed,
- anonymized aggregate stats,
- weekly case studies.

Do not expose private personal-brand work by default.

## Pricing And Packaging

Polsia's pricing teaches two lessons:

1. Low-friction trial helps people reach the wow moment.
2. Confusing credits/subscription/revenue-share language creates trust drag.

DearMe should keep pricing simpler:

- Free/demo: sample team cycle, no publishing.
- Starter: one active brand, daily letter, approval queue, limited weekly cycles.
- Pro: more cycles, imports, portfolio publishing, opportunity scout, voice memory.
- Concierge/Team: high-touch setup for founders/operators with real distribution goals.

Do not lead with credits unless the unit is obvious to a normal user.

Better unit:

> cycles per week

Because the product ritual is a cycle: plan, produce, approve, learn.

## Trust And Safety Packaging

DearMe handles identity. That makes it more sensitive than Polsia in one important way: a bad post or bad outreach email can damage the user's reputation.

So the product should package safety as a premium feature:

- approval before public actions,
- voice match before publishing,
- visible rationale,
- easy rollback,
- private by default,
- redacted public demos,
- no substrate names in UI,
- no hidden posting.

Best trust line:

> DearMe does not replace your judgment. It gives your judgment a team.

## Launch Narrative

Recommended launch story:

> I was tired of doing real work that disappeared into private chats, repos, and notes. So I built DearMe: a small AI growth team that turns my work into public proof, posts, portfolio updates, and opportunities every day. I still approve what represents me. But I no longer start from zero.

Launch artifacts:

- a live sample team page,
- Peter's own DearMe daily letters,
- before/after public profile,
- a week of approved outputs,
- opportunity pipeline screenshots,
- "what my team did while I was away" thread.

## Product Acceptance Criteria

DearMe is packaged correctly when:

- A new user describes it as "I hired a small AI brand team."
- The first screen shows work in progress, not configuration.
- Within 90 seconds, the user receives useful artifacts.
- Every public-facing action has approval by default.
- The user's voice feels protected, not replaced.
- The product never exposes Paperclip, OpenClaw, Codex, adapter, or model plumbing as primary UI.
- The daily letter creates a reason to come back.
- The demo can be understood without reading docs.
